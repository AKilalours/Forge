# Forge Voice Story

## When to Use

Use this skill when someone wants to preserve the meaning behind a passage they wrote,
prepare a short author's note, or turn a personal memory into a keepsake. It complements
Forge's Voice Story experience. It is not an AI-detection or authorship judgment tool.

## Procedure

1. Ask whether the writer wants a reflective conversation. Do not request a document or
   personal details unless they choose to share them.
2. Ask for one exact sentence or short excerpt the writer wants to keep. Preserve its
   wording and ask permission before shortening it.
3. Ask one open question at a time: what detail they remember, what they hoped the reader
   would feel, and what they want a future reader to carry forward. Let the writer skip any
   question.
4. Reflect the writer's own phrases back. Do not invent memories, emotions, relationships,
   motives, sensory details, or biographical facts.
5. Ask whether they want an assembled keepsake. Keep their wording intact by default. If
   they ask for editing, show the proposed edits separately and ask them to approve them.
6. Offer a concise title and layout only after the writer requests one. Mark any assistant-
   written title or connective wording as suggested text, not the writer's words.

## Guardrails

- Never infer who wrote a passage from a detector score, style, grammar, metadata, or a
  user's identity. A detector signal is not proof of authorship or misconduct.
- Do not tell a writer how to evade AI detection or disguise generated text.
- Do not upload, store, or share their text. If the writer wants an external analysis, name
  the provider and ask for explicit consent before any transfer.
- Do not publish, message, or send the resulting keepsake anywhere.
- Keep the writer in control of each excerpt, answer, edit, and export.

## Verification

Before returning a keepsake, confirm that its excerpt matches the writer's selected text,
that any assistant-written words are clearly identified, and that no detail was added that
the writer did not provide or approve.
